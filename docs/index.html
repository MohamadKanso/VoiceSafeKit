Local voice safety layer v0.6.0

Catch the private details speech-to-text writes as words.

Transcripts say “four two four two…” and “alex dot rivera at gmail dot com”, not digits and @ signs, so regex and NER tools miss them. VoiceSafeKit reads spoken numbers, emails and dates as written form, finds cards, one-time codes, IDs and credentials, then redacts the original transcript in your browser before an LLM sees it.

The problem nobody was checking

Speech-to-text writes what people say, not what they mean.

PII filters are built for typed text. A voice transcript spells numbers, emails and dates out as words, so they pass straight through. Here is the same transcript through a text-only filter and through VoiceSafeKit.

What speech-to-text wrote Text-only filter VoiceSafeKit
my card is four two four two four two four two four two four two four two four two SAFE, sent as-is my card is [CARD_1]
email me at alex dot rivera at gmail dot com SAFE, sent as-is email me at [EMAIL_1]
my social is four seven one, five five, eight eight four three SAFE, sent as-is BLOCK: my social is [SSN removed]
the code they just texted me is four eight two nine one three SAFE, sent as-is [OTP_1] + scam warning for the assistant
I need to build an emergency fund this year v0.4: BLOCK (false alarm) SAFE

SpokenPII benchmark

        
Measured against Microsoft Presidio.

        

          No public benchmark measured PII the way speech-to-text writes it, so the repo ships
          one: 540 labelled sentences in written and spoken form, 40 tricky everyday requests,
          and a set read aloud by a TTS voice and transcribed by Whisper.
        

      

      

        

          Spoken PII protected
          
100% vs 21%

          
270 spoken sentences: cards, SSNs, phones, emails, PINs, one-time codes, passports, dates of birth, names. With spoken mode off, VoiceSafeKit itself drops to 11%.

        

        

          Held-out real speech
          
98% vs 65%

          
108 utterances spoken by a British TTS voice and transcribed by Whisper small.en, frozen before any change and never tuned on. The 2 misses are public as the next fixes.

        

        

          False alarms, everyday requests
          
2% vs 42%

          
1 of 40 everyday requests flagged on real speech ("set a timer for twenty five minutes", "call Sue"), 0 of 40 on the synthetic set. About 0.06 ms per sentence against 3 ms, with zero dependencies.

        

      

      

        Honest notes: the synthetic set and the detector share an author, so it is a regression
        suite more than proof; Presidio was run with its defaults and is not built for speech.
        Reproduce with python benchmarks/run.py --presidio.

Drop it into your voice agent

        
The LLM sees placeholders. The voice says the real thing.

        

          Pipecat, LiveKit, Open WebUI and LiteLLM drop-ins, plus audio bleeping for recordings.
          Reversible placeholders keep personal data out of the model while the agent can still
          say it back. Passwords, PINs, CVVs and one-time codes are never stored, so they can
          never be read aloud.
        

      

      
User says    : Hi, my name is Alex Rivera. My card number is four two four two four two four two
               four two four two four two four two and my email is alex dot rivera at gmail dot com.
LLM receives : Hi, my name is [NAME_1]. My card number is [CARD_1] and my email is [EMAIL_1].
TTS says     : Thanks Alex Rivera. I'll send the receipt to alex dot rivera at gmail dot com.

guard_in, restore_out, guard_ctx = (

    guard_ctx, assistant_aggregator,
])
